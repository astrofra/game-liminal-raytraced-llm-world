Scene title for context only: Les six directions / Six directions.
A roofless human survey installation built to investigate a completely invisible labyrinth, surrounded by endless flat ochre terrain. Six nearly identical pairs of slender square gray survey posts stand around a small central open patch, arranged as a broad hexagonal constellation, each pair framing an apparently identical distant horizon. Each post pair stands on one very low rectangular slab; there are no connecting walls, lintels or roof. Six short broad rectangular slabs point outward from the central patch like a broken compass rose, with wide gaps between them; they do not form a solid circular disc. Three distant split rectangular pylons provide deliberately ambiguous alignments, with the same repeated silhouette. At the bottom edge in foreground a small rectangular open equipment tray assembled from five boxes and one short luminous white survey block are the only personal traces. Crucially the scene beyond the posts is visually uninterrupted: the original alien walls cannot be seen in any way. No glass, ghost outlines, force fields, wall shadows or transparent labyrinth diagram. Camera just above crouching height, oblique broad perspective from one edge of the central patch so the near posts frame the far pairs, spatial disorientation within wide open visibility. Six forks outward, all equally plausible, no marked correct route. Gray geometry, ochre ground and expansive yellow-green sky; subtle sky bounce, low sunless lighting. Around 30 boxes with some rotated in plan.
