Scene title for context only: Le jardin des mirages / The garden of mirages.
An architectural reinterpretation of the story's hallucination-producing plants: an abandoned geometric garden of SEVEN strange rigid branching structures made only from rectangular gray blocks. Each has a thick upright square trunk about four meters tall and just three or four very broad thin rectangular blades attached at different heights, tilted and rotated as rigid plates, like impossible industrial fronds or gigantic angular fans. These are obviously minimal architectural sculptures, not biological detailed plants. No curved leaf shapes, bark, veins or flowers. The structures stand on low square footings along a narrow dry sunken straight walk, ochre lower floor bordered by two low rectangular ledges. The plates overlap in perspective, creating unsettling repetitions and apparently inconsistent vanishing directions while all perspective is physically coherent. One structure leans slightly as a whole and its attached plates differ in angle from the others; this is fixed geometry, not melting, motion blur or optical special effects. A narrow distant open doorway between two gray blocks anchors the end of the walk. Eye level view from within the grove, large dark plate cuts diagonally into the upper left foreground, bright olive-yellow sky visible in generous jagged gaps, soft warm indirect illumination on undersides. No roof canopy enclosing everything, no long staircase or conventional courtyard. Intimate scale and perceptual unease, approximately 45–55 boxes.
